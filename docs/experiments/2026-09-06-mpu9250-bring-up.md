# MPU-9250 Bring-Up

## Objective

Establish that the Raspberry Pi can identify and read the rover's physical IMU
before using its measurements for control or system identification.

## Interface

```text
HiLetgo GY-9250       Raspberry Pi 4
VCC                   physical pin 2, 5 V
GND                   physical pin 9, ground
SDA                   physical pin 3, GPIO2 / I2C1 SDA
SCL                   physical pin 5, GPIO3 / I2C1 SCL
```

The exact product listing specifies a 3-5 V supply and an internal low-dropout
voltage regulator. The bare MPU-9250 chip is not itself 5 V tolerant; the
breakout's regulator is what makes this supply connection valid.

## Evidence

- Linux exposed `/dev/i2c-1`.
- The device acknowledged address `0x68`.
- Register `WHO_AM_I` (`0x75`) returned `0x71`, the MPU-9250 identity value.
- A 100-sample stationary run at 20 Hz measured gyroscope means of `0.695`,
  `1.254`, and `0.612` degrees/second on X, Y, and Z.
- Stationary gyroscope standard deviations were `0.099`, `0.119`, and `0.070`
  degrees/second.
- A representative acceleration vector was `(-0.094, 1.003, -0.202) g`, with
  magnitude approximately `1.03 g`, consistent with a stationary sensor under
  gravity plus mounting tilt and sensor error.

This is stronger evidence than merely measuring supply voltage: it proves the
Pi received a valid register response through the complete I2C path.

## Next Measurement

Identify which sensor axis represents rover yaw by rotating the chassis through
a known direction. Only after that measurement should the relevant bias be
subtracted and gyro rate integrated into a change in heading.
